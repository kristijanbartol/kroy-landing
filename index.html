How it works

Your chart, your garment, and where it runs out.

01 — your chart

One file, swappable

Your chart gives a range for each measurement. We build a set of bodies that land inside every one of those ranges at once, and we let the rest of the body shape vary freely, not just the four measurements the chart names. So they cover what your chart really allows, rather than what it happens to mention.

02 — your garment

Cut once, worn by all of them

We build it from whatever you can give us, a size and a fabric at one end, a full tech pack at the other. We open it out into the flat pattern a factory would cut, and put it on every one of those bodies. Everywhere on the trouser we work out how much room it gives and how far the fabric would have to stretch to close, following the fabric straight across the seams instead of stopping at each panel edge.

03 — what you get

Where it fails, and by how much

Which of those bodies the trouser does not fit, where on it the fabric runs out, and how many centimetres are missing. Hundreds of shapes in minutes, and not one physical sample.

What these bodies are

Bodies we built to fit this chart's size M. They are not a sample of anyone's customers, and nothing here says how common each shape is, so every count on this page is a count of these women and nothing more. Give us the figures for your own customers and we will weight it to them. We will not invent them.

What we do not claim

Nobody has worn these, so we will not call this validated. It is a measurement of the garment against the body, checked independently by a cloth simulation that was told nothing about how the garment was designed and lands on the same size of answer. The draping you see makes the picture. It never decides a verdict.
