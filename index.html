Method

Geometry, per triangle, on the bodies your own chart permits.

01 — your chart

One file, swappable

A size chart is a set of intervals. We build a cohort of bodies that satisfy every one of them, over sixteen shape parameters rather than the four the chart names, so the cohort spans what the chart actually allows instead of what it mentions.

02 — your garment

Cut once, worn by all of them

Built from whatever you can give us, from a size and a fabric up to a tech pack, then flattened to the pattern a factory would cut and put on every body. Per triangle we compute how much room it gives and how far the yarn must stretch to close, following that yarn through the seam rather than stopping at the panel edge.

03 — what you get

Where it fails, and by how much

Which bodies the garment does not fit, where on the garment the fabric runs out, and how many centimetres are missing. Hundreds of shapes in minutes, without a physical sample.

What this cohort is

Bodies we built inside this chart's size-M box. They are not a sample of anyone's customers and they carry no frequencies, so every count on this page is a count of them: of the bodies this chart calls a size M. Give us your own population and we weight it. We will not invent one.

What we do not claim

This is not validated against wear trials and we will not call it that. It is a geometric assessment, corroborated independently by a cloth solver that knows nothing about the design intent and agrees with it on magnitude. Draping makes the picture; it never feeds a verdict.
